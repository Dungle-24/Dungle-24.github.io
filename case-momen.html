CASE-03

Momen — Photo-Based Expense Tracker

BA / Full-Stack & UI/UX · Mar–Jun 2026 · Mobile (Flutter)

A private, close-circle photo-sharing app where a caption doubles as a spending record — built end to end by a team of five on Flutter, Supabase, and an offline-first architecture.

01Problem Statement

Sharing photos and logging spending shouldn't be two separate chores

The team scoped Momen around three everyday frictions rather than a single incident or client brief:

What problems are users facing today?

  • Privacy concerns — people want to share photos without posting too publicly.
  • Scattered memories — group photos and experiences end up spread across different camera rolls.
  • Tedious tracking — cold financial figures are tedious to log by hand, so most people stop bothering.
02To-Be Solution

Four modules, one everyday habit

Momen turns the act of taking a photo into the mechanism that solves all three problems at once:

Momen's goal

  • Private by design — a photo-sharing space limited to close friends only.
  • One memory bank — gathering group photos and experiences into a single, easy-to-revisit place.
  • Spending without the chore — turning the everyday habit of taking a photo into a natural, visual spending record.

Four core modules

  • Auth — multi-method authentication (email + password, phone OTP, email OTP, Google) and profile setup gating access to the rest of the app.
  • Feed & Camera — captures moments with custom real-time filters and shares them instantly within a close circle.
  • Spending — automatically extracts expense data from photo captions and visualizes habits via dashboards.
  • Memories — preserves shared group history with reactions and a time-capsule-style reveal mechanic.

Two mechanisms that make it work

  • Spending tracker from captions — a short debounce after typing, then a regex scan for money formats like "30k", "2.5 million", or "50000 VND"; a single photo caption can become a full spending record without a separate entry step.
  • Offline-first posting — tapping Post saves the entry to local storage immediately; with a connection it uploads right away, otherwise it queues and auto-syncs in the background, so the post feels instant either way.
Stack: Flutter · Clean Architecture (Presentation / Domain / Data) · Riverpod · Supabase (Auth, PostgreSQL, Storage) · Row Level Security · GoRouter · Android Home Widget (Kotlin)
EXIF metadata is stripped from every photo before upload, and Row Level Security enforces access control at the database layer. Post-course roadmap: AI-assisted comment suggestions, real-time feed updates, spending leaderboards, and a native iOS widget.
03User Stories & Acceptance Criteria

Written from the feature set

The team scoped features directly rather than keeping a formal backlog. Reframed as user stories, three representative examples:

"As a user, I want to add a caption with an amount and category to my photo, so my spending is tracked automatically without a separate entry step."

Acceptance criteria — typing a recognizable money format (e.g. "30k", "2.5 million", "50000 VND") in the caption auto-fills the amount and category on the spending entry before the post is confirmed.

"As a user, I want my post to go through even without internet, so I don't lose the moment while waiting for a connection."

Acceptance criteria — tapping Post always saves the entry to local storage first; with no connection, it queues and uploads automatically once connectivity returns, with no extra action from the user.

"As a user, I want to share photos only within a close circle, so I can be more open without worrying about a public audience."

Acceptance criteria — posts are visible only to the user's confirmed circle by default, and EXIF location and device metadata are stripped before the photo is stored.
