CASE-01

Company Recruitment Management System

BA / Scrum Master · Mar 17 – May 15, 2025 · Hybrid Agile/Waterfall

A web-based recruitment platform built to replace manual, paper-driven hiring for a simulated internal enterprise client — from requirement elicitation through UAT, covering six core modules end to end.

01Problem Statement

Manual hiring had no visibility

Traditional recruitment processes are inefficient, time-consuming, and error-prone due to manual workflows and lack of automation. Businesses face delays in candidate screening, challenges in tracking application progress, and limited visibility into recruitment performance.

Who felt it

  • HR Department — the end users running job postings and candidate screening by hand.
  • Recruitment Manager — needed pipeline visibility that spreadsheets couldn't give.
  • Candidates — indirect users with no way to track where their application stood.
02As-Is Analysis

How hiring actually worked

Before touching a solution, the team spent the first week of the schedule on requirement analysis, a system workflow study, and a gap analysis — reviewing the recruitment process materials provided by the client stand-in to understand how job postings, approvals, and candidate evaluation actually happened.

What that surfaced

  • Job posting approvals ran through informal, undocumented back-and-forth — no structured workflow.
  • Candidate status lived in scattered files, not a shared, trackable source of truth.
  • Every status change or interview invite meant a manual email — no automated notifications at all.
That last gap — no automated notifications — is what turned into a concrete requirement: integrate email and Google Calendar so the system, not a person, sends the update.
03To-Be Solution

Six modules, scoped to fit 61 days

Requirements were prioritized with MoSCoW to protect the timeline: Candidate Management and Job Position Management were must-haves; extras like custom report generation were pushed out. The resulting scope:

In-scope modules

  • Candidate Management — create, update, and track candidate profiles, resumes, and statuses.
  • Job Position Management — create, publish, and manage job postings with approval workflows.
  • Recruitment Process Management — interview scheduling, candidate evaluation, stage-based tracking.
  • Analytics & Reporting — KPI dashboards for time-to-hire, cost-per-hire, applicant funnel.
  • Integration — email notifications and Google Calendar for scheduling.
  • User Management — role-based access control and secure authentication.

Explicitly out of scope

Mobile app, AI-based CV evaluation, payroll integration, and post-hiring HR functions — cut to keep the 4-person team inside the 2-month timeline.

Stack: ReactJS · Node.js / Express.js · MongoDB · Google Calendar API · Nodemailer · JWT + RBAC

What it looked like

04User Stories & Acceptance Criteria

From the Jira backlog

Requirements were written as Agile user stories and prioritized with MoSCoW. Three representative examples from the backlog:

"As an HR Admin, I want to track candidate status in a dashboard, so I can monitor the recruitment pipeline effectively."

Acceptance criteria — the dashboard shows total candidates, open job count, and scheduled interviews at a glance, updated in real time as records change.

"As an HR Admin, I want to publish a job posting through an approval workflow, so postings are reviewed before going live."

Acceptance criteria — a posting cannot be submitted without title, salary range, job type, and education/experience fields; it stays in a pending state until approved.

"As a Candidate, I want to see my application move through stages, so I know where I stand in the process."

Acceptance criteria — each application shows its current stage (Applied → Shortlisted → Interview → Decision), and the candidate is notified by email on every stage change.
05Supporting Artifacts

How the work was planned and tracked

Scope and schedule were managed with a Work Breakdown Structure, an Activity-on-Node critical path, and a Gantt chart, coordinated across a four-person team.

Deliverables

  • Requirements Document, UI Prototype (Figma), Testable Website, Test Report, Final System.

UAT ran against all six in-scope modules before sign-off. Full source is on GitHub.
